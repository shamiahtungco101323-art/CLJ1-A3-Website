FIRST PRELIM

Historical Development and Principles of Criminal Justice

Study note: Read each topic completely. The sections below are arranged so important terms and ideas are not compressed into one short paragraph.

Criminal Justice

Criminal justice refers to the system through which society responds to acts considered criminal. It involves different institutions and processes that work together in the administration of justice.

The criminal justice system is commonly discussed through its different pillars or components. Each component performs a particular function, but the components are connected because the actions of one part can affect the work of the others.

The Pillars of the Criminal Justice System

  1. Law Enforcement – responsible for preventing crime, maintaining peace and order, investigating offenses, and apprehending persons suspected of violating the law.
  2. Prosecution – evaluates criminal complaints and represents the State in criminal actions before the courts.
  3. Courts – exercise judicial power and determine cases brought before them according to law.
  4. Corrections – deals with persons who have been convicted and are serving the penalties imposed by the law.
  5. Community – plays an important role in crime prevention, cooperation, rehabilitation, and the broader administration of justice.

Principles of Criminal Justice

Criminal justice is based on legal rules and procedures. The administration of justice requires that criminal cases be handled according to law and that the rights of persons involved in the process be respected.

Due Process

Persons involved in criminal proceedings must be treated according to established legal procedures.

Presumption of Innocence

A person accused of a crime is treated as innocent until guilt is established according to law.

Right to Counsel

An accused person has the right to legal assistance in accordance with the Constitution and applicable law.

Rule of Law

Government action and the administration of criminal justice must operate within the authority and limits of law.

Important Terms to Remember

Crime – an act or omission punishable by law.

Criminal Justice System – the organized process and institutions involved in responding to crime.

Accused – a person against whom a criminal action has been brought.

Victim – a person who suffers harm or injury as a result of a criminal act.

Justice – the fair administration and application of the law.

SECOND PRELIM

Concepts and Philosophy of Prosecution

Concept of Prosecution

Prosecution is an important part of the criminal justice process. It concerns the institution and handling of criminal actions in behalf of the State. Prosecutors evaluate complaints, determine whether the available evidence supports the filing of a criminal case, and represent the People in court.

The prosecution works with law enforcement agencies while maintaining its own legal functions. Investigation and prosecution are related, but they are not exactly the same function.

Role of the Prosecutor

  • Receives and evaluates criminal complaints.
  • Examines the evidence submitted in support of a complaint.
  • Conducts or participates in proceedings required by law.
  • Determines whether the evidence supports the filing of a criminal action.
  • Represents the State in criminal proceedings before the court.
  • Presents evidence and arguments during the prosecution of a case.

Prosecution and Law Enforcement

Law enforcement officers investigate reported offenses, gather information, preserve evidence, and identify persons who may have committed an offense. The prosecutor then performs the legal evaluation required for prosecution.

Cooperation between investigators and prosecutors is important because criminal cases require evidence that is legally sufficient and properly presented.

Preliminary Investigation

Preliminary investigation is a proceeding to determine whether there is sufficient ground to engender a well-founded belief that a crime has been committed and that the respondent is probably guilty thereof and should be held for trial.

It is an important stage before a criminal case proceeds to trial when preliminary investigation is required by the Rules of Court and applicable law.

Purpose of Preliminary Investigation

  1. To determine whether a criminal case should be filed in court.
  2. To protect persons from the inconvenience and expense of a trial when the evidence does not support prosecution.
  3. To determine whether the evidence gives sufficient basis for holding the respondent for trial.
  4. To allow the parties to present their respective sides according to the applicable procedure.

Important Distinction

Investigation

Primarily concerns the gathering, preservation, and development of information and evidence relating to an alleged offense.

Prosecution

Concerns the legal evaluation and presentation of a criminal case in behalf of the State.

MIDTERM

The Court Pillar

Source topic: The Court and the historical development of the judiciary, including the judicial systems before and during the Spanish and American periods, the Supreme Court, judicial power, and types of courts.

What is a Court?

A court is a body to which the public administration of justice is delegated. It is a tribunal or forum established by law and authorized to hear and determine cases at the appropriate time and place.

In criminal cases, the court determines the issues presented before it according to the Constitution, laws, rules, and evidence properly submitted in the proceedings.

Historical Development of the Judiciary and the Court

01

Judicial System of the Pre-Spanish Filipinos

Before Spanish colonization, there were no written laws in the modern sense. Rules were derived from customs, usages, and traditions. Judicial authority was exercised by the datu, who acted as a judge in settling disputes.

02

Judicial System under the Spanish Regime

During the Spanish period, the judicial system was organized under Spanish colonial authority. King Philip II of Spain established the Real Audiencia de Manila, which performed judicial as well as executive and administrative functions.

03

Judicial System under the American Occupation

The judicial system was reorganized during the American period. Existing laws and courts were recognized and new institutions were established as part of the development of the Philippine judicial system.

04

Establishment of the Supreme Court of the Philippines

Act No. 136, enacted in 1901, provided for the organization of the courts in the Philippine Islands and formally established the Supreme Court of the Philippines.

05

Supreme Court under the 1973 Constitution

The 1973 Constitution changed several aspects of the governmental system, including the organization of the judiciary. The membership of the Supreme Court was increased to fifteen: one Chief Justice and fourteen Associate Justices.

06

Supreme Court under the Revolutionary Government

On March 25, 1986, President Corazon Aquino abolished the 1973 Constitution through Proclamation No. 3 and put in place a provisional constitutional arrangement. Provisions concerning the judiciary were adopted during this transitional period.

07

Supreme Court under the 1987 Constitution

The 1987 Constitution continues to vest judicial power in the Supreme Court and such lower courts as may be established by law. Judicial power includes the duty of the courts to settle actual controversies involving rights that are legally demandable and enforceable.

Judicial Power

Judicial power includes the duty of the courts of justice to settle actual controversies involving rights which are legally demandable and enforceable, and to determine whether or not there has been a grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction on the part of any branch or instrumentality of the Government.

Power of Judicial Review

Judicial review is the power of the Supreme Court to determine whether laws passed by Congress and acts of the President are in accordance with the Constitution when the constitutional issue is properly raised before the Court.

Types of Court

  1. Higher Court
  2. Lower Courts
  3. Collegiate Court
  4. Regular Courts
  5. Special Courts

Regular Courts

1. Supreme Court (SC)

The Supreme Court is the highest court in the Philippines. It is composed of the Chief Justice and fourteen Associate Justices. It is the final arbiter of judicial issues and may sit en banc or in divisions as provided by the Constitution and law.

2. Court of Appeals (CA)

The Court of Appeals is an appellate court that reviews decisions brought before it from the lower courts and other bodies as provided by law. It is composed of a Presiding Justice and Associate Justices.

3. Regional Trial Court (RTC)

Regional Trial Courts exercise the jurisdiction granted to them by law, including original and appellate jurisdiction over specified civil and criminal cases.

4. Metropolitan Trial Court (MeTC)

Metropolitan Trial Courts exercise the jurisdiction assigned by law in metropolitan areas.

5. Municipal Trial Court (MTC)

Municipal Trial Courts exercise the jurisdiction assigned by law in municipalities and other areas covered by their respective courts.

6. Municipal Circuit Trial Court (MCTC)

Municipal Circuit Trial Courts serve areas composed of municipalities grouped together pursuant to law and exercise the jurisdiction provided by law.

Important Court Concepts

Higher Court

A court with authority to review decisions or exercise jurisdiction as provided by the Constitution and laws.

Lower Court

A court exercising the original or other jurisdiction assigned to it by law, subject to the applicable appellate structure.

Collegiate Court

A court in which cases may be decided by several members rather than by a single judge.

Special Court

A court created or designated by law to handle particular classes of cases or specialized matters.
