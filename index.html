02

The Court Pillar

A court is a body officially assembled under the authority of law to administer justice. In the Criminal Justice System, the court determines the case brought before it according to law.

Historical Development of the Judiciary

Pre-Spanish

There were no written laws. Customs, usages, and traditions guided disputes, with the datu acting as a judge.

Spanish Regime

King Philip II established the Real Audiencia de Manila, which had judicial and other governmental functions.

American Occupation

The judicial system developed during the American period, including the reestablishment of the Real Audiencia and the appointment of Cayetano L. Arellano.

1901

Act No. 136 provided for the organization of courts and formally established the Supreme Court of the Philippine Islands.

1973 Constitution

The judicial system transitioned under the 1973 Constitution, with the Supreme Court having 15 members.

1986–1987

After the 1986 transition, a provisional constitutional framework was used, followed by the 1987 Constitution and its provisions on judicial power.

Key idea: Article VIII, Section 1 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution provides the foundation for judicial power.
03

Introduction to Court

Legal Foundation

Judicial power is vested in one Supreme Court and in such lower courts as may be established by law.

Judicial Power

It includes settling actual controversies involving legally demandable and enforceable rights and determining grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction.

Judicial Review

The power of the Supreme Court to determine whether laws and certain acts of government are consistent with the Constitution when properly raised before it.

Special Courts

Court of Tax Appeals

Handles specified tax and customs appeals and related cases within its jurisdiction.

Sandiganbayan

Handles specified criminal and civil cases involving graft and corruption by public officers and employees.

Shari'a Courts

Handle cases involving Muslim personal laws within their legal jurisdiction.

Family Courts

Handle cases assigned to them under the Family Courts Act and related laws.

Drugs Courts

Specialized courts established for specified violations under the Dangerous Drugs Act, subject to legal jurisdiction.

Intellectual Property Courts

Designated Regional Trial Courts handling specified intellectual property violations.

04

Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction refers to the power or authority given by law to a court or tribunal to hear and determine certain controversies. Venue refers to the geographical area in which a court with jurisdiction may hear or determine a case.

Subject Matter

Jurisdiction over the kind of case. The lesson connects this with the penalty provided by law.

Territory

The case must be connected to the place where the court has territorial jurisdiction.

Person Accused

The court obtains authority over the accused through lawful means such as arrest or voluntary submission.

Groups of Jurisdiction

1. Criminal

Power and authority to hear cases that are criminal in nature.

2. Civil

Power and authority to hear cases that are civil in nature and not criminal acts.

Classification of Jurisdiction

General Jurisdiction

Power to adjudicate controversies except those expressly withheld from the court.

Special Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction limited to particular cases and subject to limitations provided by law.

Original Jurisdiction

Power to take judicial cognizance of a case instituted for judicial action for the first time under conditions provided by law.

Appellate Jurisdiction

Authority of a higher court to re-examine a lower court's final order or judgment on appeal.

Exclusive Jurisdiction

Power to adjudicate a case to the exclusion of other courts at that stage.

Concurrent Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction shared by different courts under applicable law.

Delegated Jurisdiction

Authority granted to inferior courts to hear and determine specified cases under certain conditions.

Territorial Jurisdiction

The geographical area within which a court's power may be exercised.

05

Court Proceedings

1

Arraignment

The accused is informed in open court of the complaint or information and enters a plea.

2

Pre-Trial

A conference before trial that may include plea bargaining, stipulations, admissions, marking evidence, and waiver of objections.

3

Trial

The formal examination of the matter in issue before a competent court to determine guilt or innocence.

4

Judgment

The court's adjudication stating whether the accused is guilty or not guilty.

5

Appeal

The case may be elevated to a higher court for review when allowed by law.

6

Decision

The judgment rendered by a court of justice or another competent tribunal.

Search warrant reminder: The lesson explains that before issuing a search warrant, the judge must personally examine the witness under oath and reduce the examination to writing in the form of searching questions and answers.
06

Contents of Judgment

Judgment of Conviction

When the judge finds the accused guilty, the judgment addresses matters such as the legal qualification of the offense, circumstances, participation, penalty, and applicable civil liability or damages.

Judgment of Acquittal

When the judge finds the accused not guilty, the judgment states whether the prosecution failed to prove guilt or failed to prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt.
